The dot is the whole idea.
mb. opens, and the full stop lands on the i.

One mark, two states. Resting, it is mb. — three characters, square enough for a favicon. Touched, it opens into madeboring and the full stop flies up to become the tittle over the i. Nothing is added and nothing is thrown away; the punctuation you already had turns out to have been part of the word the whole time. Below are five ways to time that, then the colour decision.

Five executionsall five draw first · pivot is my pick

Every one of these now opens the same way: m is drawn as an outline, then b, then the dot, and only once the mark has been built does it fill in and open into the word. What differs is the drawing speed, whether the beat is eased or mechanical, and what the letters and the dot do afterwards. Hover to play, click to replay, Loop to watch one on repeat, Slow to see what the eye is actually catching, and Draw first to switch the prelude off and compare against the straight open.

01

Trace

the mark draws itself, then opens1.15s draw · 0.95s open

The longest, most deliberate draw of the five — a full 1.15s with a wide gap between the m and the b, so you watch each letter being made. Afterwards the word opens gently and the dot takes the highest arc. This is the one for a loading state or the first paint of the homepage, where you have the time to spend.

02

Unpack

the letters were hiding behind m and b0.85s draw · 0.90s open

A quick, even draw, then the opposite idea: ade and oring are uncovered by a wipe that opens outward from the seam between the two letters, so it reads as though the full name was always there and mb. was just the closed state. The dot takes the flattest path of the five.

03

Typeset

letter by letter, mechanical0.90s draw · 1.05s open

The draw itself is linear — no easing at all — and the m and b are spaced a clear beat apart, so it reads as a plotter rather than a hand. The middle letters then land one at a time on that same fixed beat. This is the most boring of the five in the way the brand means it — it looks like a machine setting type, not a designer showing off. Pairs with the spec-sheet voice already on the site.

0.00s
04

Pivot

the dot leads and the word appears under it0.95s draw · 1.10s open

The draw is tight and the letters come almost together, because the interest is in what happens next: the dot launches and every letter resolves as the dot passes over it, so the dot is doing the work rather than arriving as an afterthought. It is the one execution where the payoff and the mechanism are the same gesture.

0.00s
05

Stamp

one beat, no ceremony0.45s draw · 0.55s open

The draw is compressed to 0.45s and the open to 0.55s, so the whole thing is done in a second flat. Everything arrives together and the dot lands last with a single squash. This is the one that survives being triggered forty times a session in a sticky nav without becoming irritating.

Forest shipping now

The palette already on madeboring.com. Nothing here is wrong — it is just quiet enough that the site reads as a document rather than a product.

Field recommended

The green stops being an accent and becomes the ground. Cream type on a deep field, amber only on things you can click. Almost nobody in this category commits to a coloured field, which is exactly why it will not read as another AI landing page.

Signal replit energy

Near-black with one hot orange. It is the most immediately modern and the most dangerous — orange on black over a dev-tool site is Replit’s exact signature, and you will be read as derivative by the audience you most want.

Bone the pure version

Bone, ink, and a single vermilion hairline. The most literal reading of the name and the hardest to get wrong. It asks the typography and the spacing to carry everything, with nothing to hide behind.

Why Field, in one paragraph

The name is a joke about restraint, so the palette should not be shouting. But the live site is currently so quiet it reads as a document rather than a product — that is the real problem, and it is a contrast and type-size problem more than a hue problem. Field fixes it without volume: it takes the green you already own and makes it the ground instead of the trim, which reads as confident rather than loud. Amber appears only on things you can click, so the one warm colour on the page always means the same thing.

Signal is the risk. Orange on near-black over a dev-adjacent site is Replit’s signature, and the engineers you are selling to will place it instantly. Borrow Replit’s type scale and density — that is what actually makes their site feel expensive — not their hue.
